> Built with `story-development.md` §3 (Character Arcs), §3.4 (techniques) and the §12.2 card. Matches `premise.md` v0.7 (about 8,000 words, five chapters, each opened by a worksheet question).
> **Short-story rule:** only Nell, Theo, Elly and Immy carry scenes. Everyone else is a single line or a single beat (see §1.1). Where the cards below mention "Wk" (lockdown weeks), use the chapter map in §1.2.
> **Names, ages and genders are placeholders**, assumed: Lead 1 is a woman and Lead 2 a man (the child calls him "Dad" in the last line).
> Items marked **[VERIFY]** need checking; everything in the "private truth" column is author-only knowledge (never stated outright in the book).

## 2. Lead 1: Nell Brannigan

### 2.1 Card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Job** | Senior policy officer, **risk and assurance**, in a federal department (Barton/Parkes). She writes the paragraph in the brief that begins "The likelihood of harm is assessed as…". |
| **Home** | Flat 4.12, level 4, **Telopea House** (fictional), Kingston. One bedroom, a balcony facing the lake, a kitchen with labelled containers. |
| **Want** | To stay safe; to keep her life predictable |
| **Need** | To choose someone without needing a crisis to force the choice |
| **Wound** | **Her father died suddenly** when she was 12 (an undiagnosed heart condition). Her mother never stopped scanning for the next catastrophe. Nell concluded that risk is the monster and that foresight is love. |
| **Lie she believes** | *"If I follow the rules, nothing can hurt me."* |
| **Gift** | Attention to detail; steadiness under pressure; fierce loyalty; dry wit |
| **Flaw (with a cost)** | Rigid and quietly judgemental. She treats spontaneity as negligence, and she will hurt Theo with it. Late in the story she *quantifies* him. |
| **Contradiction** | A professional assessor of risk who makes the single riskiest choice of her life in a typed memo |
| **Defining habit** | Notes the time of everything on a plain steel watch; labels her food containers; keeps a paper planner even though she has an app |
| **Signature phrase** | *"Technically…"* |
| **Object** | The paper planner, with Immy's name crossed out and Theo's written in pencil |
| **Mirror** | A pot of herbs she keeps alive by schedule; it dies the one week she stops watering it (the week of the Notice) |
| **How she speaks** | Precise, qualified, apologetic ("I just think", "to be fair"). Under stress, longer and more formal. Her love language is a typed list. |
| **What she will sacrifice** | Her control; the plan; the story she tells about herself |
| **Arc (lie → truth)** | From *safety as love* to *risk as love*, and, if the frame is right, **back to safety** once the crisis stopped forcing her. Proof by action: she goes down the stairs (Ch. 3), and, years later, the frame hints she shuts the door. |

### 2.2 Nell at 31 vs. Nell at 41

| | 31 (inner story) | 41 (frame) |
|---|---|---|
| **Stance** | Cautious but curious | Controlled, protective, tired |
| **Humour** | Dry, mostly internal | Dry, aimed at her own editing |
| **Relationship to the story** | Living it | Curating it for a child |
| **Verbal tic** | "Technically" (then a pause) | "I was careful." (a word she knows is not the whole answer) |

### 2.3 Scenes that prove her arc
| Scene | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **The Note** (Ch. 1) | She breaks her plan and her pride in the formal register she trusts |
| **First dinner** (Ch. 2) | She sits down without wiping the table |
| **The declaration** (Ch. 2, 14 Sept) | She says it first, as if reading a finding, and times the pause |
| **The Notice** (Ch. 3) | She sees the lie, then goes down the stairs anyway |
| **The release** (Ch. 5) | She writes down the time he says "Staying", and does not say what she feels |
| **The frame** (2031, every chapter) | She edits the story for the child; the edit marks show (three uses only: Ch. 1, 3, 5) |

### 2.4 Voice sample (frame, first person, present tense; style §2.1)
> *Elly has a pencil behind her ear, which she thinks makes her look like a journalist. The worksheet is on the table between us, flattened with the heel of her hand. Question four is circled twice.*
> *"Were you scared?"*
> *"Start at the beginning," I say. "Technically, that's the rule."*

### 2.5 Voice sample (inner story, close third, past tense; the edit device)
> *Nell was careful. She was careful in the way that other people were tall: it was simply how she was built.*
> *"Careful" is the word I gave Elly. There is another one.*

## 3. Lead 2: Theo Marangos

### 3.1 Card

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Job** | Sous-chef at **Dom's restaurant** in Braddon, stood down at lockdown; takeaway/delivery shifts; **about to start a sous-chef job in Hobart** |
| **Home** | Flat 2.07, level 2, **Telopea House**. A studio with one good knife roll, a hot plate, and four boxes already taped shut. |
| **Want** | To keep moving; to leave before he can be left |
| **Need** | To stay with something when it stops being easy |
| **Wound** | **His father left** when Theo was 14 (a family bakery failed; he went to Perth and sent postcards). Roula held the family together by cooking. Theo learned that people leave and that the one who goes first controls the pain. |
| **Lie he believes** | *"If I don't commit, I can't be left."* |
| **Gift** | Warmth; improvisation; he feeds people and turns a pantry into an occasion |
| **Flaw (with a cost)** | **Evasive.** He hides bad news until it is forced. He has two concealments: the Hobart job (from the start) and the exposure alert (a day). |
| **Contradiction** | Lavish with food and attention, miserly with disclosure |
| **Defining habit** | Sings under his breath on the stairs (old Greek songs, then Elvis, then nothing); pinches salt with three fingers; asks "what are we eating?" before "how are you?" |
| **Signature phrase** | *"Ela, sit."* (Greek: "come, sit"); a joke when he's scared |
| **Object** | The knife roll, and a **sourdough starter named Barry** that must be fed daily |
| **Mirror** | Barry the starter: it can't be left alone, it forgives neglect for a while, and it dies if no one feeds it |
| **How he speaks** | Loose, funny, deflecting. Humour is his exit door. Short answers when frightened. |
| **What he will sacrifice** | The Hobart job (or, as the reader may suspect, not quite) |
| **Arc (lie → truth)** | From *leaving first* to *staying through fear*; the frame hints at a **return to the old pattern** once there was nothing forcing him. Proof by action: hides the alert (old), stays through quarantine (new). |
| **Voice on the page** | **Documents only**: texts, voice-notes, a recipe card, a list taped to a cupboard. His interior stays closed. |

### 3.2 Theo's documents (style §11)
Examples to write into the book:
- **Voice note, 6:03 am**, Week 7: *"Hey. It's me. Never mind. I'll tell you later."* (unsent)
- **The recipe card** he leaves for Nell: ingredients and a one-line instruction: *"Don't stir it. Trust the heat."*
- **The list on the cupboard:** *1. flour 2. Barry 3. call Mum 4. tell Nell (Hobart).* (The last item is never crossed out.)
- **A text** the night of the release: *"Staying. Is that okay?"*

### 3.3 Scenes that prove his arc
| Scene | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **The knock** (Ch. 1) | He says yes, and does not say he was leaving |
| **First dinner** (Ch. 2) | He cooks for someone for the first time in months |
| **The declaration** (Ch. 2) | A beat too long: the old reflex |
| **The Notice** (Ch. 3) | He reads the alert at 6:02 am and says nothing |
| **Quarantine** (Ch. 4) | He tells the truth about Hobart when the email arrives |
| **The release** (Ch. 5) | He texts "Staying. Is that okay?": the new pattern, or the old one wearing a different coat |

## 4. The two leads together

| Dimension | Nell | Theo | In the bubble | Outside it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Relation to risk** | Avoids it | Seeks it | Each steadies the other | Each resents the other |
| **Relation to truth** | Over-discloses | Under-discloses | Her honesty rewards him; his concealment hurts her | A pattern |
| **Fear** | Of harm | Of being left | Both look like love | Both look like a mismatch |
| **What each offers** | Safety | Joy | A trade | A debt |
| **What each hides** | That he was her *second* choice | That he was *leaving* | The origin of the bond is a double half-lie | The ending |
| **Symmetry** | She chose from need, not preference | He stayed from constraint, not choice | Each can tell the story in which they *chose* and the other was only available | The reader cannot settle it |

**The central design:** each person is exactly what the other lacks and exactly what the other fears. In the bubble, that looks like love. Outside it, it might still be.

## 6. Supporting cast (one sharp function each; §3.4)

### 6.1 Immy Doyle: the failed nominee, the theme voice
- **Function:** says the theme line once; embodies the road not taken
- **Details:** a speech pathologist, tall, loud-laughing; the friend who has always said what Nell can't. Moves in with her boyfriend on lockdown day 3 (ending her single household, so the bubble is impossible), then calls daily. Reappears at the release.
- **Her line (Ch. 2, on the phone, after the 14 Sept declaration):** *"Nobody falls in love in a lockdown. They just run out of other places to be. I'm saying it because I love you."*
- **Arc note:** she is not an antagonist. She is *right* and *kind*, which is why it hurts.

### 6.2 Maureen "Mo" Brannigan: fear as inheritance
- **Function:** shows where Nell learned caution
- **Details:** lives in Sydney's inner west (in a far worse outbreak); reads the daily NSW case numbers aloud on the phone; sends articles; "I just want you safe." She does not know about Theo until week 6.
- **The scene that matters:** Nell lies to her mother by omission, and hears herself doing what Theo does.

### 6.3 Roula Marangos: love as cooking
- **Function:** Theo's origin and the cost of staying behind
- **Details:** runs a small bakery-café in Queanbeyan, 15 minutes away but in another jurisdiction **[VERIFY cross-border rules]**. Sends food via a drop-off. Knows about Hobart; says nothing.
- **Line to plant:** *"Your father also said he'd stay."*

### 6.4 Dom Ferraro: Theo's pattern, spoken
- **Function:** says what Theo cannot
- **Details:** owns the restaurant; short, tired, kind. In a phone call: *"He's good until it gets real."* He holds Theo's job and his own; he's the one who sends the takeaway shift that leads to the Notice.

### 6.5 Beryl Anstey: the community witness
- **Function:** the neighbour who sees everything; the shared experience of the city
- **Details:** 78, lives opposite Nell; knocks on the wall at 11:02 each morning to say "did you see the numbers"; leaves a jar of lemon butter on the doormat; the first person to see Theo in the corridor and say *"About time."*

### 6.6 The health voice
- **Function:** a neutral clock; no characterisation; the human drama stays between the leads
- **Form:** a text at 6:02 am: *"You have been identified as a close contact…"* **[VERIFY exact wording/process]**

## 8. The first meeting (the nomination) in detail

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| **Backstory** | Nell has lived on level 4 for three years and has seen Theo twice: once with six bags of flour, once singing *"Fly Me to the Moon"* in the stairwell at 2 pm on a Tuesday |
| **Trigger** | Immy's collapse on Day 3 |
| **The note** | Typed, one page, subject line included (see `premise.md` §6.1) |
| **The delivery** | Slid under Flat 2.07 at 8:15 pm on Day 5, then four minutes pacing |
| **The reply** | A **double knock**, quiet. A man in an apron, the note in his hand with a pencil mark on it (he has corrected a typo). *"Technically, it's 'principle', not 'principal'."* |
| **What each hides** | Nell: that he was her second choice. Theo: that he was leaving. |
| **Why he says yes** | He has just been told that Hobart is on hold "until further notice", and he is, for the first time in years, lonely in a way he can't cook out of. |
| **Why she is not as safe as she thinks** | In the stairwell, face to face, she realises she nominated the man she had noticed twice. |
