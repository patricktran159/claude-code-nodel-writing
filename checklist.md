# Quality Checklist and Review: *Close Contact*

> **Part 1** is a reusable checklist for any novel or short story, built from `story-development.md` and `writing-style.md`. **Part 2** applies it to *Close Contact* (five chapters, about 8,000 words) with evidence. **Part 3** records the strengths, weaknesses, fixes already made, and a prioritised revision plan.
> Reviewed text: `chapters/ch01` to `ch05` (after the style and romance/tragedy polish pass).

---

## Part 1. How to use the checklist

### 1.1 Rating key

| Mark | Meaning | Score |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Strong: does the job well, with clear evidence on the page | 5 |
| **G** | Good: works, with a minor gap | 4 |
| **A** | Adequate: present but thin, uneven or over-used | 3 |
| **W** | Weak: missing, misfiring or damaging | 2 |
| **X** | Absent where it was needed | 1 |
| **n/a** | Not applicable to this form | – |

### 1.2 Process
1. Read the work once without notes (what do you remember?).
2. Run the measurements (Part 2.1).
3. Go through each section, rating every item with evidence.
4. Rank weaknesses by **reader impact**, not by ease of fixing.
5. Re-run after each revision pass.

## Part 3. Findings

### 3.1 Strengths (ranked by reader impact)

1. **The concept does double work.** The worksheet frame makes the telling matter. A child's innocent either/or turns the whole story into a question. The edit device ("Careful is the word I gave Elly. There is another one.") puts the theme into the syntax.
2. **The ending.** The face-down photograph is planted in Ch. 3 ("for the light"), then paid off as "almost happy". Four bars hummed into the hallway on Sundays, with the narrator not turning around "until it stops", is a precise, small, devastating image. The final line is the child's unanswered question.
3. **Romantic set pieces that are specific to these two people.** The typed memo with "principal"; the cushion that is placed on the floor after eleven seconds; "Don't sing." / He sang; the dance counted in steps; "Is that a promise or a weather report?"
4. **Plants and payoffs.** Boxes → basement cage; "Unknown" → unchanged; herbs → dead; photograph; Barry. The reader is rewarded for attention.
5. **Characterisation through objects and habits.** The watch, the labels, the planner, the knife roll, the postcard ("Weather good here. Dad.").
6. **Dialogue with subtext.** "It's cold." (instead of "I love you"); "Noted."; "No." delivered "in under a second. I have timed it since."
7. **Humour that keeps the sentiment honest.** Colander vs. magpies; "That's the whole confession."; the saucepan that is banged "three times, and then thought better of it."
8. **Clean prose.** Almost no filler; no filter-verb clutter.

## Part 4. Verdict

*Close Contact* is a distinctive, tightly built short story. Its premise, frame device, plants and payoffs, and final image are the strongest elements and work as designed. The main craft risk is **rhythm**: the prose is consistently shorter and more clipped than the style guide's baseline. The main structural gap is **Nell's flaw**, which is under-dramatised and makes the later reversal less earned. The loss at the end would also hit harder with one concrete glimpse of what was lost. All three are inexpensive to fix and would move the overall score from about 4.3 to about 4.6 out of 5.
