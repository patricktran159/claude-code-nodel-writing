## 2. Premise card

| | |
|---|---|
| **Universal situation** | Two people fall in love when nothing else is available. |
| **Constraint / twist** | The lockdown lets each of them see exactly one other household. It also cancels the thing that would have ended it: he was about to leave town. |
| **Clock** | Nine weeks, announced a few at a time. Inside it, a 14-day quarantine ✅. |
| **Logline** | *In Canberra's 2021 lockdown, a woman whose job is to measure risk nominates the quiet stranger two floors down as her one permitted visitor. He was days from leaving town and finds the world has closed behind him. | **Second question** | Did he stay, or was he kept? |

## 3. Form (how it is told)

### 3.1 Structure: five chapters, each opened by a worksheet question
Each chapter begins with a **frame passage (80–150 words)**: Nell and Elly at the kitchen table, Elly reading one worksheet question. Nell's answer becomes the chapter. The last chapter ends with the frame (about 350 words) and Elly's final, unscripted question. **Frame total: about 900 words; inner story: about 7,100.**

### 3.2 Voice
- **Frame (about 2031): first person, present tense.** Dry, short, protective (`writing-style.md` §2.1).
- **Inner story (2021): close third person, past tense, on Nell,** as she chooses to tell it.
- **The edit device (the story's signature).** When older Nell edits for her child, the narration shows the cut: *Nell was careful.* ⟶ *"Careful" is the word I gave Elly. There is another one.* The reader sees both words and decides. Use it **only three times in the whole story** (Ch. 1, Ch. 3, Ch. 5), so it stays sharp.
- **Theo speaks through documents, not POV** (style §11): texts, voice-notes, a recipe card, a list. His interior stays closed. Cap: **one document per chapter** (Ch. 1: the pencil correction on the Note; Ch. 2: the recipe card; Ch. 3: the unsent voice-note; Ch. 4: the Hobart email; Ch. 5: the text "Staying. Is that okay?").

### 3.3 Why the structure serves the theme
A story about whether fear was mistaken for love is told by someone choosing, line by line, which word to use for her own fear.

## 4. The private truth (author's bible; never stated outright; v1 timeline in §4.1)

1. **Theo had a one-way offer.** A sous-chef job in Hobart, starting mid-September 2021, was the plan. He told no one. The lockdown held the job "until further notice"; the offer stayed open until 1 Nov **[VERIFY plausibility]**.
2. **Nell nominated Theo only because her first choice collapsed.** Immy moved in with her boyfriend on day 3, ending her single household and the bubble.
3. **After the lockdown** (15 Oct) Theo stayed. They moved in together in December. Their daughter Eleni ("Elly") was born in August 2022.
4. **The end.** No affair, no cruelty. Around 2025, with Elly three, **Nell re-armoured**: risk registers, schedules, quiet vetoes. Theo's old pattern, leaving first, did what it always did. They separated in 2026. Each can tell it so that the other left.
5. **The reversal:** the careful one may be the one who ended it, and the man who "stayed because he couldn't leave" kept choosing for five years after the lockdown stopped forcing him.
6. **None of this is confirmed in the text.** Nell's unspoken answer: *"Both of us. Neither of us. I don't know."*

### 4.1 Causal timeline after the lockdown (what really happened, and why)

| When | What happens | Why (the cause) |
|---|---|---|
| **15 Oct 2021** | Theo stays. The Hobart offer lapses on 1 Nov. | For the first time, leaving would cost him something. He had never stayed anywhere; nothing about *him* had changed except the price of going. |
| **Nov 2021** | Nell is pregnant. A contraceptive failure: the woman who assesses risk for a living lands on "low probability, high consequence". | The irony she cannot make a spreadsheet of. |
| **Dec 2021** | Theo moves up two floors into Nell's flat. Barry the starter comes with him. The lease on his studio had ended. | Practical, not romantic. The stairwell becomes a staircase inside a home. |
| **Mar 2022** | They marry at a registry, two witnesses (Immy, Roula). Nell proposes by memo; Theo replies, *"Technically, yes."* | A callback to the Note, and the happiest page of the real story. |
| **Aug 2022** | Eleni (Elly) is born. | |
| **Feb 2024** | Elly, 18 months, has a **febrile convulsion**. Six hours in the hospital; she is fine. Theo is on a closing shift at Dom's and arrives late. Nell remembers *him absent* for the worst of it. | **The cause of the re-armouring.** Her father's sudden death returns. She concludes (again) that foresight is survival, and that she was alone with it. This is real fear, not paranoia, which is why neither can call her a villain. |
| **2024–25** | Nell begins a **household risk register** on the fridge door: the pool gate, the stairs, the car seat. And, in a column she doesn't show him, Theo: *"Likelihood of leaving: Unknown."* (the same word she gave him in her planner in Ch. 1, and she never changed it). | Fear that moves from the world to the person. The loop: she watches for him leaving, so he feels watched, so he deflects with jokes, so she records another warning sign. |
| **Sept 2025** | Dom opens a second restaurant; Theo takes the night shifts. The last argument is about a Tuesday school pick-up. Neither can remember what was said. | The old pattern, wearing a new coat: Theo's exit is humour and absence. The new pattern, Nell's: a list. Each performs the other's fear. |
| **Mar 2026** | Theo moves "for a bit" into the flat above Roula's bakery in Queanbeyan. Neither files anything; "for a bit" never ends. | Nobody makes the decision. That is the tragedy: a separation with no moment of choice. |
| **2026–31** | Co-parenting, Sunday handovers. **On Sundays Theo still comes in and feeds Barry** at Nell's bench; they do not discuss it. | The last shared chore. It is what Elly sees. |
| **Oct 2031** | The worksheet. Elly has been told only that "Mum and Dad live in two houses." | The frame. |

### 4.2 The logic that makes the ending fair
- **Neither is a villain.** Nell's fear is rooted in a real death (her father) and a real scare (the convulsion). Theo's pattern is rooted in a real abandonment (his father) and he **stayed for four and a half years after the constraint lifted**, which is the best evidence in the story that his staying in 2021 was also a choice.
- **The fear-loop mirrors the lockdown.** In 2021, shared fear bound them. In 2024–26, *separate* fear drove them apart. The same force, reversed.
- **The two questions get answered by the structure, not the narrator.** *Did he stay or was he kept?* He stayed for years once nothing kept him. *Was it love or fear?* The author's view: both, and the child's either/or is too small. Nell's refusal to answer *is* the answer.
- **What the reader may infer** (never stated): they are separated; the fault is shared; Nell may have started it; Theo still comes on Sundays.
- **What stays unknowable:** who made the first move to end it; whether it could have gone differently; whether the lockdown love was "real".

### 5.0 How to fit the story into 8,000 words
- **Ch. 1 (1,400):** three beats only: the plan and the planner (with the risk table), Immy's collapse (by text), the Note and the knock. No backstory beyond one line about her father and one about the flour.
- **Ch. 2 (1,900):** **two scenes plus a montage.** Montage (about 500 words, summary style, per `writing-style.md` §9.1): five weeks of dinners, the 11 am press conference, magpies, the stairwell. Scene A (about 600): the declaration on 14 Sept. Scene B (about 400): Immy's call and the theme line. Close on a single paragraph: the tulips.
- **Ch. 3 (2,100):** one continuous day (6:02 am to 7 pm to the stairs). The longest chapter; the hinge.
- **Ch. 4 (1,400):** two scenes: the Hobart email (about 700) and the swab/negative result (about 500). A short quarantine summary bridges them.
- **Ch. 5 (1,200):** the balcony at midnight (about 700), then the frame close (about 350) with the last question.

## 6. Key scene cards (§7.2)

### 6.1 The Note: Ch. 1
| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Nell: find a bubble partner without admitting she needs one |
| **Obstacle** | Pride, rules, a near-stranger |
| **Turn** | A memo: *"Subject: Request to nominate (single's bubble). Context: my first nominee is unavailable. Rationale: you appear to be a low-risk individual who lives alone. I saw you carry six bags of flour up the stairs on Thursday. This is not a romantic proposal."* |
| **Outcome** | A double knock; he has corrected a typo in pencil (*"principle", not "principal"*) |
| **Object** | The note, kept, and (possibly) shown to Elly at the end |
| **Plant: the risk table** | In her paper planner, a four-row table rating possible nominees. *Immy: 0.2. Colleague: 0.4. Theo: Unknown.* She picks "Unknown". The same word returns on the fridge in 2024–25 (§4.1). |

### 6.2 The declaration: Ch. 2 (14 Sept ✅)
After the month-long extension, Nell says "I love you" in her careful voice, *as if reading a finding*. Theo laughs, then goes quiet. The beat is too long. She notes the time on her watch. Immy, later, on the phone: the theme line.

### 6.3 The Exposure Notice: Ch. 3 (the hinge)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Theo: keep the relationship. Nell: stay safe, in control |
| **Obstacle** | His fear of being sent away; her fear of risk |
| **Turn** | He reads the alert at 6:02 am and tells himself he will tell her after breakfast. At 7 pm she sees it on his phone. |
| **The choice** | Door-drop from two floors up (safe, correct) or walk down the stairs |
| **Outcome** | She goes down. She does not forgive him. She goes down anyway. |
| **Ambiguity** | Her reason is never given |
| **Plants** | The stairwell (the "border"), the labelled containers, the watch |

### 6.4 The Hobart email: Ch. 4
In quarantine, Theo's phone buzzes on the bench with a subject line from a Hobart kitchen. Nell reads it before he can reach for it. The scene turns on *what she does next*: not shout, but quietly **put down a labelled container** and ask, *"When were you going to tell me?"* His answer is the second concealment confirmed and, perhaps, a gift.

### 6.5 The release: Ch. 5
Midnight, 15 Oct. They sit on the balcony and watch lights come on in other flats. For nine weeks a rule has kept them in; now none does. He texts (from two metres away): *"Staying. Is that okay?"* She writes down the time.

### 6.6 The last page: Ch. 5 (frame)
Elly, pencil behind her ear: *"Is that why you and Dad aren't together? Because you were only scared then?"* End.

## 9. Setting: the Canberra texture bank

### 9.1 The lockdown, as lived ✅
- Began 5 pm, 12 Aug 2021, as a seven-day lockdown; extended repeatedly; ended 11:59 pm Fri 15 Oct.
- **Daily 11 am press conferences** by the Chief Minister (Andrew Barr).
- Masks required for everyone aged 12+ outside the home, including outdoors (except when exercising).
- Leaving home only for essentials: shopping, health care, essential work, exercise (limits changed; by late Sept, up to two hours a day).
- Hospitality: takeaway and delivery only; shops closed.
- **The single's bubble:** a person living alone could nominate one other household to visit and receive visits from.
- **Exposure sites:** time-windowed; close contacts quarantined 14 days and got tested. *(Use a fictional venue.)*
- **Floriade 2021 cancelled** as an event, but bulbs were planted and planters distributed; the flowers bloomed without crowds.
- **15 Oct:** cafes, restaurants and pubs reopened at limited capacity; retail not until 29 Oct.
- ACT vaccination was very high (about 99% first dose of eligible residents; about 75% fully vaccinated near the end).

### 9.2 Place and weather
- **Spring:** frosty mornings, warm afternoons; **magpie swooping season**; wattle; blossom; light on Mt Ainslie.
- **Telopea House** (fictional block, Kingston): four floors; a concrete stairwell with an echo; a bin room; a rooftop clothesline; the lift one person at a time.
- **Seen at a distance:** Parliament House flag, Black Mountain Tower, Lake Burley Griffin.
- **The border:** Queanbeyan is 15 minutes away but in NSW; Theo's mother lives there **[VERIFY cross-border rules]**.

### 9.3 Domestic textures
Labelled containers; a grocery spreadsheet; masks in a bowl by the door; the sourdough starter; flour; taped lines on the floor; hand sanitiser on the bench; laundry on the balcony; the 11 am press conference on the laptop; case numbers on the fridge; chalk tally marks.

## 12. Risks and answers (§13)

| Risk | Answer |
|---|---|
| Tragedy left to the last page | A warm, funny middle; the Notice gives a real betrayal and cost; the Hobart email doubles it |
| Idealised leads | She is rigid and judgemental; he is evasive and doubly concealing |
| Coincidence in the nomination | A *planned* nominee collapses; the replacement is someone she had already noticed |
| Over-explained theme | Theme line spoken once, by Immy; never restated |
| Short form rushes the romance | Ch. 2 compresses five weeks into one set of rituals; each ritual must *change* something |
| Pandemic cliché | The story is small: two flats and a stairwell; no wards, no death counts |
| Edit device overused | Three uses only (Ch. 1, 3, 5) |
| 8,000 words is tight | Strict chapter budgets (§5.0); summary for time, scene only for turns; no scene without a turn |
| Backstory (private truth) bloats the text | Never narrated; only seeded (§8) and implied by the last page |
